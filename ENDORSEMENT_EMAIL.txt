A short summary of the paper (6 pages, 3 figures, 4 tables):

  Title: Distribution-Preserving QIM: An Invisible Watermark that
         Preserves the Natural DCT Marginal Distribution

  Abstract (one paragraph): Recent black-box removal attacks against
  invisible image watermarks (notably UnMarker, IEEE S&P 2025) succeed
  by detecting the statistical fingerprint that quantization-index-
  modulation watermarks leave on the DCT coefficient distribution. We
  propose Distribution-Preserving QIM, which replaces the uniform QIM
  lattice with a CDF-partitioned lattice whose cells carry equal
  natural mass and whose target values are sampled from the natural
  distribution's safe interior. Against a reimplemented UnMarker-style
  attack, our method retains >=89% bit accuracy at settings that drop
  the classical DCT-QIM baseline to 77% -- a +12 to +14 percentage-
  point survival advantage, reproduced on both synthetic and real
  images. The full benchmark harness including the attack is open-
  source at github.com/sairam782/Invisible-Watermarking.
