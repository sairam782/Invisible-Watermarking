================================================================

Recent black-box removal attacks against invisible image watermarks
(notably UnMarker, S&P 2025) succeed by detecting the statistical
fingerprint that quantization-index-modulation (QIM) watermarks leave on
the marginal distribution of DCT coefficients. We propose
Distribution-Preserving QIM (DP-QIM), which replaces the uniform QIM
lattice with a CDF-partitioned lattice whose cells carry equal natural
mass and whose target values are sampled from the natural coefficient
distribution restricted to a safety-margin interior of each cell. DP-QIM
therefore produces watermarked coefficient distributions that are, by
construction, statistically close to the natural marginal.

Headline result: against a reimplemented UnMarker-style peak-density
attack, DP-QIM retains >=89% bit accuracy at settings that drop the
classical DCT-QIM baseline to 77% -- a +12 to +14 percentage-point
survival advantage at identical attack parameters, reproduced on both
a photo-statistics-preserving synthetic benchmark and a nine-image real
photo set (where the advantage is +12.3pp at matched attack strength).
On a standard attack suite DP-QIM further wins Gaussian blur (+28 to
+36pp), downscale (+27 to +37pp), and heavy JPEG (+3 to +9pp) at
matched or better PSNR.

We characterize the (undetectability, robustness) Pareto frontier via
an autoresearch loop that automatically searches the DP-QIM
hyperparameter space (369 trials, 31-config Pareto frontier in 5 min on
CPU) and release the full benchmark harness including the
UnMarker-class attack module at
https://github.com/sairam782/Invisible-Watermarking .
