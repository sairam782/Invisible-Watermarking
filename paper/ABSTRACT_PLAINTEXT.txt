TITLE
=====
Distribution-Preserving QIM: Histogram Detectability and Robustness Trade-offs in Invisible Watermarking

PLAINTEXT ABSTRACT
==================
Quantization index modulation (QIM) leaves a lattice fingerprint in DCT coefficients. We study Distribution-Preserving QIM (DP-QIM), which replaces a mismatched carrier with a sample from a target quantile cell rather than a lattice point. We derive an idealized cellwise mixture formula and a positive-scaling property restricted to a zero decision boundary; neither guarantees exact distribution preservation or image-level robustness. Corrected experiments use 43 crops from 14 deduplicated photographs, one outer source split for all detector fitting and calibration, and conditional source-bootstrap intervals. At 64 bits, learned-histogram detector AUC is 0.65 [0.63, 0.76] for DP-QIM versus 0.95 [0.91, 1.00] for QIM; DP-QIM's AUC rises to 0.84 at 256 bits and 0.96 at 1,024 bits. DP-QIM is therefore detectable, not statistically invisible. At the same DCT position and interpolated matched PSNR, it gains approximately 35, 33, and 21 percentage points in bit accuracy under blur, half-resolution downscaling, and half-contrast processing, respectively. It loses approximately 16 and 29 points under JPEG qualities 90 and 70, 14 under additive noise, and 3 to 7 under a simplified histogram-jitter attack. These small-sample results characterize a robustness/detectability trade-off, not security against strong steganalysis or the original UnMarker attack. Code and correction records are released at https://github.com/sairam782/Invisible-Watermarking.
